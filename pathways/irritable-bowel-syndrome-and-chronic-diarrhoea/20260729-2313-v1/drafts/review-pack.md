# IBS and chronic diarrhoea - combined pathway review pack

**Run:** 20260729-2313-v1

**Audience:** NHS Dumfries & Galloway GPs, ANPs and community clinicians

**Status:** Comparison draft for local clinical/editorial review

**Date:** 29 July 2026

## Why merge the pathways?

The separate IBS and chronic diarrhoea drafts repeated much of the same primary-care assessment:

- alarm features and lower gastrointestinal cancer assessment;
- coeliac testing and inflammatory markers;
- qFIT before selective faecal calprotectin;
- infection and medicine review;
- deciding between positive IBS diagnosis and gastroenterology referral.

The combined pathway uses one front door, followed by a short clinical-decision section that separates positive IBS management from persistent diarrhoea requiring specialist investigation.

## Proposed structure

1. Shared primary-care assessment
2. Shared initial investigations
3. Clinical decision after results
4. IBS management
5. Referral and non-referral criteria
6. Secondary-care investigations

## Important messages retained

- HIV serology is part of the initial GP investigation of chronic diarrhoea.
- Faecal calprotectin is not a routine test for every possible IBS presentation.
- qFIT comes before calprotectin where lower gastrointestinal cancer is being assessed.
- A normal work-up plus Rome IV symptoms supports a positive IBS diagnosis.

## Potential disadvantage of merging

The combined page is longer. However, it should reduce contradictory advice and make the testing sequence easier to maintain. Accordions preserve quick access to the relevant branch.
