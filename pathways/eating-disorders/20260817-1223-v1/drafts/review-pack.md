# Eating disorders in adults — review pack

## Purpose

This draft turns the supplied local documents into a primary-care-facing DGRefHelp page. The November 2025 ICP is the main local source. The Copilot summary was useful for structure but was checked against the full ICP. The 2008 enhanced-service specification and its risk form were treated as historical material, not current clinical authority.

The draft was checked against:

- Scottish Government, *National Specification for the Care and Treatment of Eating Disorders in Scotland* (2024)
- SIGN 164, *Eating disorders* (revised August 2022)
- Royal College of Psychiatrists, *Medical Emergencies in Eating Disorders (MEED)* (2022)
- NICE NG69, *Eating disorders: recognition and treatment*

## Overall assessment

The November 2025 ICP is broadly aligned with current guidance in recognising a wide range of eating disorders, the importance of early intervention, multidisciplinary care, MEED, specialist dietetic and psychological input, and medical/psychiatric liaison.

However, important parts appear to have been carried forward from the 2008 enhanced-service specification. The BMI-led risk form, fixed monitoring intervals, admission thresholds, DEXA policy, refeeding detail, activity ladder and some engagement/discharge wording should not be published unchanged. The DGRefHelp draft replaces these with current national guidance and flags local decisions still needed.

## Material inconsistencies and recommended action

| Area | Supplied local material | Current guidance | Recommended resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status of enhanced service | The specification started in February 2008 and states an end date of 31 March 2010. It contains historic payment arrangements and old epidemiology. | It is not a current clinical or contractual standard. | Label as historical and retire it from active clinical use. Do not reproduce payment, incidence or mortality claims on DGRefHelp. |
| Scope and terminology | The 2008 document centres on anorexia, bulimia and “atypical” disorders; the newer ICP adds BED, OSFED and ARFID. | The Scottish specification covers AN, BN, BED, OSFED and ARFID across ages, genders and body sizes, with equitable access. | Use inclusive current terminology and state explicitly that serious illness can occur at normal or higher BMI. |
| Screening | The ICP describes SCOFF as validated and uses EAT/BITE/EDE-Q in parts of the pathway. | NICE says screening tools such as SCOFF must not be the sole method of determining whether an eating disorder is present. | Retain SCOFF only as a brief aid. Do not use a score to exclude referral. Keep specialist questionnaires out of the primary-care gate. |
| Referral threshold | The Copilot summary and ICP can be read as leaving “low risk” cases mainly in primary care. The local route is via CMHT, then possible SEDS input. | NICE recommends immediate referral to an age-appropriate eating-disorder service once suspected. The Scottish specification promotes early access, specialist-service self-referral and referral from relevant professionals; access must not depend on BMI. | Refer all suspected adult cases promptly through the currently commissioned local route. Confirm whether direct professional/self-referral to SEDS is now available. Do not require deterioration or a low BMI before referral. |
| Risk assessment | The supplied form starts with BMI bands and increases a categorical risk level for selected behaviours. Pregnancy, breastfeeding or insulin-dependent diabetes are automatically labelled high risk. | Scottish guidance and MEED require a holistic, multidimensional assessment. BMI is one factor and must not be used alone. Diabetes, pregnancy and other comorbidities require specialist joint pathways rather than a simple category. | Replace the form with a MEED-aligned assessment/checklist and clinical formulation covering physical, psychiatric and social risk, trajectory, behaviours, capacity and support. |
| Temperature and circulation thresholds | The legacy form rates temperature below 35°C as moderate and below 34°C as very high; systolic BP below 80 mmHg or pulse below 40 as very high. | MEED identifies adult temperature below 35.5°C, waking pulse below 40, and systolic BP below 90 with syncope/significant postural fall among red factors. | Use MEED red/amber criteria and trajectory. Do not wait for the more extreme legacy thresholds. |
| Biochemical admission criteria | The ICP gives a narrow “in extremis” list, for example potassium below 2.8 only with bicarbonate above 38, sodium below 125 plus confusion/seizures and glucose below 2.8. | MEED uses a broader traffic-light assessment: severe or changing electrolyte abnormalities, hypoglycaemia, renal/liver abnormalities and clinical context may require urgent care. The MEED checklist uses thresholds intended to trigger concern before the legacy “in extremis” values. | Replace the standalone local biochemical list with MEED and acute-care protocols. State that normal or previously normal bloods do not exclude danger. |
| Admission decisions | The ICP includes BMI below 13 as a psychiatric admission criterion and uses “high/very high” categories. | NICE says not to use an absolute weight or BMI threshold for day/inpatient admission. Medical versus psychiatric setting depends on the person’s physical and psychiatric needs and the service’s competence. | Use BMI below 13 only as one MEED red indicator. Base setting and urgency on the full assessment and joint medical/psychiatric discussion. |
| Monitoring intervals | The 2008 LES prescribes fixed intervals by low/medium/high risk. The ICP carries forward six-monthly, six-weekly, fortnightly and weekly schedules. | The Scottish specification requires MEED-based protocols, an explicit owner for clinical responsibility, named responsibility for tests/results/escalation and monitoring responsive to change. Primary care should not hold sole responsibility for significant/severe illness without specialist agreement. | Remove generic fixed schedules. Create an organisational shared-care protocol and individual monitoring plan. Document who orders, performs, reviews and acts on every element. |
| Bone health | The LES suggests routine DEXA, including repeated scans; the ICP says baseline DEXA for all with BMI below 17.5 and contains an old drug-treatment algorithm. | NICE advises considering DEXA after 2 years of underweight in adults, or earlier for bone pain/recurrent fractures, and not repeating more often than annually without those symptoms. Weight restoration is the main treatment. | Remove BMI-triggered routine baseline DEXA and the legacy drug algorithm. Use NICE criteria and seek specialist osteoporosis/endocrine advice. |
| Refeeding | The ICP appendix reproduces old calorie ranges, electrolyte doses and monitoring from a 2008 BMJ article/NICE nutrition guidance. | MEED emphasises expert-led refeeding, monitoring for refeeding syndrome and avoiding harmful underfeeding. Regimens depend on risk and clinical setting. | Do not publish detailed refeeding prescriptions on the primary-care page. Link to MEED and a locally approved acute/community refeeding protocol. |
| Activity | The ICP includes an inpatient activity ladder based mainly on BMI and elsewhere encourages weight-bearing exercise above BMI 16. | Exercise advice should consider medical stability, compulsive exercise, osteoporosis and individual formulation. NICE advises stopping excessive exercise and avoiding high-impact activity with osteoporosis/fracture risk. | Retire the BMI-only ladder from the referral page. If an inpatient activity protocol is needed, rewrite it as a separately governed MDT protocol. |
| Psychological treatment | The ICP focuses on MANTRA and repeatedly states that the person must be motivated/ready to change. | SIGN/NICE support a range of diagnosis-specific treatments. The Scottish specification requires proactive outreach, flexible engagement and avoidance of barriers caused by ambivalence. | Present MANTRA as one option, alongside CBT-ED and SSCM for adult AN and relevant BN/BED interventions. Reframe ambivalence as a treatment need, not an exclusion criterion. |
| Programme requirements and discharge | SEDS “non-negotiables” may lead to a person being asked to leave; re-referral is suggested only after about six months. | The Scottish specification calls for assertive follow-up, prevention of inappropriate discharge, holistic and collaborative discharge, relapse planning and direct re-engagement within an agreed timeframe. Discharge should not be based solely on behaviour or clinical measures. | Retain necessary safety boundaries but require formulation, outreach, risk review and an alternative care plan before discharge. Review the blanket six-month re-referral delay. |
| Transitions | The ICP describes CAMHS/adult transfer but mostly as a risk-based hand-off near age 18. | The Scottish specification recommends early planned transition, joint working where appropriate, written plans and no return to a waiting list for active cases. | Add explicit ownership, joint planning and continuity requirements, including physical monitoring during transition. |
| Document control | The full ICP has blank approval fields and dates that appear internally inconsistent/future-dated. | DGRefHelp requires clear governance and review metadata. | Confirm authorship, approval status, implementation date, version and review date before publication. |

## Local governance questions before publication

1. Is the commissioned adult referral route still GP/professional referral to CMHT by SCI Gateway, with CMHT deciding SEDS involvement?
2. Can professionals or patients now refer directly to SEDS, as envisaged by the Scottish national specification?
3. What are the local response standards for routine, urgent and same-day eating-disorder referrals?
4. Which service holds clinical responsibility while a person waits, and who reviews and acts on observations, bloods and ECGs?
5. What exact route should primary care use for same-day medical assessment, out-of-hours advice and mental-health crisis?
6. Is there a current D&G MEED form/checklist that should replace the supplied BMI-led form?
7. What locally approved acute and community refeeding protocols should be linked?
8. Are there agreed joint pathways for diabetes, pregnancy/postnatal care, ARFID/neurodivergence and CAMHS-to-adult transition?
9. Are SEDS programme criteria, attendance expectations and the six-month re-referral statement still current?
10. Which service leads bone-health decisions and interprets DEXA results?
