# Eating disorders LES update review

Prepared 5 October 2026 for discussion with primary care commissioning, the LMC, psychiatry/SEDS and acute medicine.

**Yes. The supplied LES needs a substantive clinical and operational update.** Retain its central purpose: remunerated physical monitoring in general practice as part of multidisciplinary care. Replace the clinical appendices and agree a clear shared-care protocol. Updating DGRefHelp alone will not amend the LES or establish new practice obligations.

The supplied PDF is dated 25 February 2008, with a service period of 1 February 2008 to 31 March 2010 (section 4). That is evidence about this copy, not proof that the service ceased in 2010. ## Changes needed in the LES

| Priority and location | Finding in the supplied LES | Recommended update |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate correction — appendices 3 and 5 | Both forms print BMI as height squared divided by weight. Visually confirmed on PDF pages 7 and 10. | Correct to **weight (kg) / height (m)²**. Check any electronic templates derived from these forms. |
| High — appendix 4 | Risk starts with BMI bands, then adds categories for selected behaviours. This can give false reassurance at normal/higher weight. | Replace with a locally approved MEED-aligned assessment covering trajectory, observations, intake, behaviours, biochemical/ECG findings and mental health risk. Do not translate old risk categories directly into MEED colours. |
| High — appendix 4 and emergency interface | The old thresholds and “discuss abnormalities with RMO” wording do not provide a usable current escalation process. | Specify immediate/same-day action, named receiving services and out-of-hours routes. Do not wait for extreme abnormalities, symptoms accompanying every abnormal result, or multiple abnormal parameters. Use current MEED alongside acute protocols. |
| High — appendix 1 | Fixed schedules: low-risk examination six-monthly, bloods three-monthly and ECG annually; medium-risk examination 6–8-weekly, bloods 4–6-weekly and ECG eight-weekly; high-risk checks fortnightly. | Replace automatic schedules with an agreed patient-specific plan, linked to current risk and change. Frequent or unstable monitoring may require specialist/acute provision rather than a routine practice appointment. |
| High — sections 3 and 4, appendix 1 | The existing requirement for agreement with secondary care is useful, but sending forms onward does not adequately define responsibility for interpreting and acting on results. | Specify named clinical responsibility, task allocation, results review, acknowledgement, escalation, cover and non-attendance response, including while waiting for assessment and after discharge. |
| High — appendix 5 | The review blood panel lists FBC/U&Es/LFTs/Mg/Ca/amylase/TFTs but omits explicit phosphate and glucose. | Agree a current baseline and repeat-test protocol with laboratory and specialist teams, explicitly addressing phosphate, magnesium, potassium and glucose when clinically relevant. Avoid automatic repetition of every baseline investigation. |
| Medium — appendix 1 | DEXA appears on a routine two-yearly schedule, with a low-risk qualification if osteopenic on the initial scan. | Use an indication-based bone-health pathway. For adults with anorexia nervosa, NICE recommends considering scanning after two years of underweight, or sooner for bone pain/recurrent fractures; persistent underweight may justify repeats. Specify who requests and interprets scans. |
| Medium — sections 1 and 2 | Age 18+ scope is explicit, but terminology and epidemiology are dated. | Preserve a clear adult scope, modernise diagnostic terminology and remove unsupported historical incidence/mortality figures. Specify provision for BED, OSFED and ARFID and the under-18/transition interface. |
| Commissioning — sections 4, 6 and 7 | Old dates, broad competence wording and historical per-contact fees. | Agree current term, workforce competence, training, equipment, capacity and funded activities. Cost result interpretation, liaison, non-attendance work, urgent repeat checks and administrative time as well as face-to-face contact. Do not assume the ICP creates a funded GP service. |
| Evaluation — section 5 | Counts of monitored patients, defaults and admissions are proposed. | Retain useful activity data; add overdue monitoring, time to action abnormal results, completion of care plans and transfer/relapse outcomes. Review non-attendance as a potential risk signal. |

The risk and shared-care changes are supported by [MEED CR233](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/improving-care/campaigning-for-better-mental-health-policy/college-reports/2022-college-reports/cr233) and outcome 5 of the [Scottish national specification](https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-specification-care-treatment-eating-disorders-scotland/). Bone-health recommendations are in [NICE NG69, 1.10.12–1.10.18](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng69/chapter/recommendations#physical-health-assessment-monitoring-and-management-for-eating-disorders).

## The newer psychiatry documents also need reconciliation

The full ICP is the local source; the Copilot summary is a secondary summary, not an independent guideline or evidence of approval. These points are separate from the LES findings above:

- **Document control:** despite its “Nov 25” filename, the ICP records last review November 2026 and next review November 2027. Approval, status and implementation fields are blank. November 2026 is future-dated relative to this review. Confirm the intended version and approval status.
- **Access:** the opening flowchart directs low-risk presentations to PCMHN/self-management resources. That must not become a barrier to specialist assessment when an eating disorder is suspected. Retain supportive primary care input while arranging assessment.
- **Conflicting monitoring:** the ICP low-risk table uses six-monthly bloods and ECG, whereas the LES uses three-monthly bloods and annual ECG. The medium/moderate schedules also differ. Neither should silently supersede the other in a contractual document.
- **Medical admission:** section 11 uses very late “in extremis” criteria, including temperature below 34°C, systolic BP below 80 mmHg, potassium below 2.8 with bicarbonate above 38, and sodium below 125 with confusion/seizures. These are not safe prerequisites for seeking acute care. The revised page uses earlier MEED assessment/escalation prompts.
- **Risk form:** the separately supplied form largely reproduces the ICP appendix. It should be replaced alongside the LES form so clinicians are not given competing systems.
- **Bone health, refeeding and activity:** the ICP retains baseline DEXA for BMI below 17.5, an older osteoporosis drug algorithm, detailed refeeding prescriptions and a BMI-led inpatient activity ladder. These require separate specialist review; they are not reproduced as primary-care instructions.
- **Engagement and relapse:** programme readiness criteria must be distinguished from access to assessment and treatment. Review discharge for non-adherence and the request for about six months away before re-entry to SEDS. Provide proactive reassessment and an alternative care plan when a particular programme is unsuitable.
- **Local routes:** CMHT and SEDS roles and GP access to dietetics are described, but no SCI Gateway instruction is present in the supplied text or initial flowchart. The previous workspace draft asserted that route; the new draft marks the actual referral method for confirmation.

The Scottish specification also sets national treatment-access expectations of four weeks for routine cases and one week for urgent cases (outcome 3.10), and promotes self-referral. These are national expectations to reconcile with local commissioning, not verified local booking guarantees. They do not replace emergency assessment.

## Proposed replacement clauses for discussion

These are drafting proposals, not an agreed service specification or determination of existing contractual duties.

**Purpose and scope.** The service funds agreed physical-health assessment and monitoring for adults with an eating disorder as part of a multidisciplinary care plan. Define eligibility, practice participation, specialist acceptance and access for people awaiting assessment. Preserve the existing statement that the specification does not redefine essential or additional services.

**Acceptance and ownership.** Before ongoing enhanced monitoring begins, the specialist service and participating practice agree a written plan with the patient. The plan names the clinician/service holding overall responsibility, the tasks accepted by each provider, next review date, and arrangements during leave, non-attendance, deterioration and transfer. A referral or discharge letter alone does not constitute acceptance of a new monitoring arrangement.

**Monitoring and results.** Specify each measurement/test, its frequency, the person arranging and performing it, the clinician interpreting it, the time for review and the route for communicating action. Record trajectory as well as individual results. Abnormal or missing results trigger documented follow-up. Changes in risk prompt revision of the plan rather than waiting for the next scheduled visit.

**Escalation and capacity.** Provide a named same-day specialist/acute contact and out-of-hours route. If the required intensity cannot safely be delivered in general practice, the specialist and commissioning services arrange an appropriate alternative. Routine LES monitoring is not a substitute for emergency assessment or specialist-led refeeding.

**Funding and assurance.** Negotiate fees against the agreed workload, including test interpretation, ECG review, liaison and failed-contact work. Define claim rules, staff training, audit measures, annual review and a process for updating clinical appendices when national guidance changes.
