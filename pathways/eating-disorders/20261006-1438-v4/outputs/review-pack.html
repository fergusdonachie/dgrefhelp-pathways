Eating disorders V4 — generalist pathway review

Draft for local clinical agreement, 6 October 2026. Supersedes the V3 pathway for review; the historical LES assessment is carried forward unchanged.

What changed

The page now follows the first GP consultation: recognition, focused physical assessment, on-page escalation and prompt referral for diagnosis/treatment. Investigations are proportionate to physical concerns, with local referral requirements to be confirmed. Detailed specialist assessment and nutritional prescriptions are outside its scope.

Initial assessment does not start a fixed monitoring programme. Follow-up for unresolved symptoms/abnormal results remains necessary; continuing surveillance while waiting needs an explicit interim agreement. The after-diagnosis section is separate and brief. This distinction is proposed local operational wording, not a national prohibition on monitoring before diagnosis.

Live GP examples informing the structure

  • NHS Tayside RefGuide: Eating Disorder Service (Adult): Initial referral information and explicit agreement on ongoing monitoring; local exclusions/routes not transferred.
  • Cornwall RMS adult eating disorders: Consultation-to-referral structure and differential assessment. Thresholds and monitoring policy not copied.
  • Mersey Care adult eating disorders: professionals: Recognition and sensitive questions; not used as sole authority for risk thresholds.

These examples use different eligibility and monitoring arrangements. Cornwall also recommends monitoring for some patients outside its referral criteria; it does not support a blanket rule against review before diagnosis. NICE NG69 1.2.10 supports prompt referral of suspected cases after initial assessment. Scottish national specification outcome 5 supports clear written responsibilities while waiting and shared-care agreements.

Clinical and local review

The adult escalation criteria are carried forward from the V3 source check against MEED Table 1 (printed pp31–35) and Appendix 3. They are a conservative GP action aid, not a reproduction of red/amber categories or admission criteria: checklist biochemistry, isolated QTc prolongation and significant postural changes prompt same-day advice; multiple concerning findings prompt same-day discussion. Local agreement on implementation remains needed. MEED is a reference, not a required second document during consultation.
