## Key clinical thresholds

- **Faecal calprotectin:** <50 = normal (diagnose IBS); 50–120 = equivocal (repeat in 4 weeks; NSAIDs can cause false positive); >120 = raised (refer to gastroenterology)
- **Rome IV criteria:** Recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week on average in last 3 months, associated with ≥2 of: (1) related to defecation; (2) change in stool frequency; (3) change in stool form — with symptom onset ≥6 months ago
- **Alarm feature age threshold:** Age >45 with new/changed bowel symptoms warrants investigation regardless of IBS features; age 16–45 without alarm features can be assessed against Rome IV
- **TCA dosing:** Start amitriptyline 5–10 mg nocte; titrate up to 30 mg; review at 4 weeks then 6–12 monthly

## Editorial uncertainties / notes for editor

1. **NICE CKS not fetched (403).** Recommendations derived from NICE CG61 (2008, updated 2017) plus QURIS pathway. CKS may contain more current guidance — manual check recommended before publishing.
2. **BSG 2021 guidelines not accessible.** Full BSG/Gut guideline (Lacy et al. 2021, doi:10.1136/gutjnl-2021-324598) was behind paywall. Recommendations on ondansetron, linaclotide, and prucalopride are based on QURIS pathway content and NICE CG61. Check BSG for any updated first-line thresholds.
3. **Linaclotide/Prucalopride:** Marked as Specialist Initiation Only per QURIS pathway. NICE CG61 specifies linaclotide only after laxatives have failed AND ≥12 months constipation. This restriction is reflected in the pathway.
4. **Amitriptyline/Nortriptyline:** Unlicensed for IBS — flagged with note in drug section. Informed consent required per NICE CG61.
5. **Ondansetron:** Used in IBS-D in secondary care; included in diarrhoea section with BNF link. Not explicitly licensed for IBS — may warrant an unlicensed note. Verify local formulary position.
6. **Low FODMAP:** Listed as dietitian-supervised only, consistent with NICE CG61 and D&G local context.
7. **Psychology referral:** NICE CG61 recommends after 12 months refractory IBS. D&G equivalent of IAPT — recommend editor confirms current referral route (PCMHT or self-referral).
8. **Rome IV vs NICE CG61 criteria:** NICE CG61 uses slightly older criteria (pain relieved by defecation OR associated with altered frequency/stool form). QURIS uses Rome IV. This pathway uses Rome IV as per QURIS — more current standard.

## Section summaries

### Background
IBS prevalence ~10–20% adults (UK); F:M ratio approximately 2:1. Four subtypes: IBS-C, IBS-D, IBS-M, IBS-U. Positive diagnosis — not exclusion of all else. Significant quality of life impact.

### Primary care assessment
Rome IV criteria + alarm feature checklist + investigation protocol (bloods + faecal calprotectin with exact thresholds).

### Primary care management
Stepped: lifestyle → dietitian → drugs by symptom type. Specific agents with doses and BNF links. Badges for specialist-initiation drugs.

### Who to refer
- Urgent/USOC: alarm features
- Routine: raised calprotectin, equivocal calprotectin not settling, failed primary care management (≥12 months), psychology

### Who not to refer
Confirmed IBS + normal investigations + no alarm features + symptoms manageable in primary care.
