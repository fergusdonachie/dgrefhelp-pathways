The acute red, hot, swollen joint (suspected septic arthritis): assessment, management and referral

Identify the patients who need same-day orthopaedic discussion, and safely manage the well patient with a likely crystal or osteoarthritis flare with early review.

  • Prosthetic joint or penetrating wound over the joint → discuss with orthopaedics on call the same day. Do not aspirate a prosthetic joint in the community and do not start antibiotics.
  • Red flags in a native joint — extreme pain on movement, systemic upset (with or without temperature), pyrexia, or inability to weight bear → same-day orthopaedic discussion.
  • Well patient, good range of movement, weight bearing, apyrexial, history of gout or osteoarthritis → bloods (FBC, CRP, ESR), analgesia, and review in 24 hours.
  • At 24-hour review: worsening → discuss with orthopaedics (if infective) or rheumatology (if inflammatory); improving → review at 4–6 weeks.
