MGUS is common, usually asymptomatic, and not cancer. It is often found incidentally and does not need treatment, but it requires risk-based monitoring because a minority of patients progress to myeloma or related lymphoplasmacytic malignancy.

The key primary-care task is to identify myeloma early, recognise CRAB features, and arrange same-day assessment when malignant spinal cord compression or acute kidney injury is suspected.
