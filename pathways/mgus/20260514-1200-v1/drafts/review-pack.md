## 1. Background

Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS) is a premalignant plasma cell disorder characterised by a low-level paraprotein (M-protein) in the serum without evidence of end-organ damage or haematological malignancy.

**Diagnostic criteria (all must be met):**
- Serum M-protein < 30 g/L
- Bone marrow clonal plasma cells < 10%
- No CRAB features (hypercalcaemia, renal impairment, anaemia, bone lesions)
- No evidence of B-cell lymphoma or related disorder

MGUS is **not cancer**. It is important to communicate this clearly to patients, while explaining that monitoring is needed because a small proportion of people do progress.

**Epidemiology:**
- Approximately 3% of people over 50 have MGUS
- Approximately 8.9% of people over 85 have MGUS
- Progression risk: approximately 1% per year to myeloma, Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia, AL amyloidosis, or lymphoma

**Types of MGUS:**
- **Non-IgM MGUS** (IgG, IgA, IgD): the majority; may progress to multiple myeloma or AL amyloidosis
- **IgM MGUS**: may progress to Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia, AL amyloidosis, or lymphoma
- **Light chain MGUS**: abnormal free light chain (FLC) ratio only; no intact M-protein detectable

## 2. Risk Stratification (Mayo Clinic / IMWG Model)

Three independent risk factors for progression:

| Risk Factor | Definition |
|---|---|
| M-spike ≥ 15 g/L | |
| Non-IgG type | IgA, IgM, or IgD paraprotein |
| Abnormal FLC ratio | Kappa:lambda ratio < 0.26 or > 1.65 |

## 3. Primary Care Assessment

### History
- **Myeloma symptoms:** bone pain (especially back and ribs), fatigue, recurrent bacterial infections, unexplained weight loss, thirst or polyuria (hypercalcaemia)
- **AL amyloidosis symptoms:** ankle oedema, macroglossia, carpal tunnel syndrome, unexplained weight loss
- **IgM MGUS / Waldenström's symptoms:** visual disturbance, headache (hyperviscosity), peripheral neuropathy

### CRAB Features — must not be missed
> ⚠️ Any CRAB feature requires urgent haematology referral (USC/2WW pathway)

| Feature | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **C**alcium | Corrected calcium > 2.75 mmol/L |
| **R**enal | Creatinine > 177 µmol/L or eGFR < 40 ml/min without other cause |
| **A**naemia | Hb < 100 g/L without other cause |
| **B**one | Lytic lesion, pathological fracture, or severe osteoporosis |

### Examination
- Lymphadenopathy
- Hepatosplenomegaly
- Pallor
- Bone tenderness
- Peripheral oedema

### Initial Investigations at Diagnosis

| Investigation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| FBC, U&E, calcium, albumin, LDH | Baseline / CRAB screen |
| Serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) | Quantify paraprotein type and level in g/L |
| Serum free light chains (FLC) | Kappa:lambda ratio for risk stratification |
| Urine Bence Jones protein | Spot urine protein:creatinine ratio; early-morning urine if raised |
| Skeletal survey | Only if symptomatic — not routinely required for MGUS |

### Ongoing Monitoring Bloods
- FBC
- U&E
- Calcium
- Serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) — to track paraprotein level

## 4. Primary Care Management

### Monitoring Intervals by Risk

| Risk Group | Initial check | Subsequent monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk (0 RF) | SPEP + FBC/U&E/calcium at 6 months | If stable, annually for 2–3 years, then every 2–3 years |
| Low-intermediate (1 RF) | SPEP + FBC/U&E/calcium at 6 months | Annually thereafter |
| Intermediate-high (2 RF) | Refer to haematology | — |
| High (3 RF) | Refer to haematology | — |

### Triggers to Refer from Primary Care Monitoring
Any of the following during monitoring should prompt haematology referral:

1. Paraprotein increase > 25% from baseline **AND** absolute increase ≥ 5 g/L within 6 months
2. Any new CRAB feature
3. New symptoms: bone pain, recurrent infections, unexplained anaemia, weight loss, hyperviscosity symptoms
4. Patient request for specialist review

### Patient Information Points
- MGUS is **not cancer**
- The vast majority of people with MGUS never develop myeloma
- Monitoring exists for early detection if progression does occur
- Patients should report new symptoms promptly — they should not wait for their next scheduled review

## 5. Who to Refer

### USC (2WW) / Same-Day Emergency Haematology
- Any CRAB feature
- Clinical suspicion of myeloma (significant bone pain, recurrent bacterial infections, hyperviscosity symptoms)
- Bence Jones proteinuria with raised urine protein:creatinine ratio

### Urgent Haematology (Non-Cancer Pathway)
- IgA or IgM paraprotein > 10 g/L
- IgG paraprotein > 15 g/L
- Paraprotein rise > 25% within 6 months (minimum absolute increase 5 g/L)
- Light chain MGUS (abnormal FLC ratio, no intact paraprotein detectable)
- Diagnostic uncertainty

### Routine Haematology
- 2–3 Mayo risk factors (intermediate-high or high risk group)
- IgM MGUS of any level — consider discussion with haematology given Waldenström's risk
- Patient request

### Information to Include in All Referrals
- SPEP: paraprotein type and level in g/L
- Serum FLC kappa:lambda ratio
- FBC, U&E, LFTs, calcium, albumin
- Urine Bence Jones protein result
- Previous SPEP results (trend is clinically important)
- Presence or absence of CRAB features
- Current symptoms

---

## 6. Who Not to Refer

- **Low risk MGUS** (IgG, M-spike < 15 g/L, normal FLC ratio), asymptomatic, stable — primary care monitoring is appropriate
- **Very elderly or frail patients** without symptoms — community monitoring is appropriate; if uncertain, discuss with haematology before referring
- **Patients not fit for or not wishing active treatment** — community monitoring; email haematology for advice if unsure
- **Do not refer solely for an incidentally discovered low-level paraprotein** — risk-stratify using Mayo criteria first
