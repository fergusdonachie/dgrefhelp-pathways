Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS)

MGUS is a premalignant plasma cell disorder characterised by a low-level paraprotein (M-protein) in the serum, without end-organ damage or haematological malignancy. It is not cancer.

Diagnostic criteria (all must be met)

  • Serum M-protein <30 g/L
  • Bone marrow clonal plasma cells <10%
  • No CRAB features (hypercalcaemia, renal impairment, anaemia, bone lesions)
  • No evidence of B-cell lymphoma or related disorder

Epidemiology

  • ~3% of people over 50; ~8.9% of people over 85
  • Progression risk: approximately 1% per year to myeloma, Waldenström’s macroglobulinaemia, AL amyloidosis, or lymphoma

Types of MGUS

  • Non-IgM MGUS (IgG, IgA, IgD): most common; may progress to multiple myeloma or AL amyloidosis
  • IgM MGUS: may progress to Waldenström’s macroglobulinaemia, AL amyloidosis, or lymphoma
  • Light chain MGUS: abnormal free light chain (FLC) ratio only; no intact M-protein detectable

Risk Stratification — Mayo Clinic / IMWG Model

Three independent risk factors for progression:

  1. M-spike ≥15 g/L
  2. Non-IgG paraprotein type (IgA, IgM, or IgD)
  3. Abnormal serum free light chain ratio (<0.26 or >1.65)
