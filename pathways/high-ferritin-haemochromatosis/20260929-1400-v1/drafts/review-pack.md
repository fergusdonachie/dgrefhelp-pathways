# High ferritin / haemochromatosis

Draft revision of the DGRefHelp "under development" page, rewritten to follow the
national CfSD Genetic Haemochromatosis pathway (Liver SDG, published September
2026; board letter 23/09/2026). The page carries numbered **[TO AGREE n]**
placeholders. Each one maps to a decision in the log below, and all of them need
agreeing locally before publication.

## Key principle

Most raised ferritin is not iron overload. Act on ferritin as possible iron
overload only when Tsat is >45%. GH means C282Y homozygosity **plus** biochemical
iron overload. Refer only C282Y homozygotes with iron overload, and unexplained
ferritin persistently ≥1000 ng/mL despite lifestyle change. Everyone else is
managed in primary care.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lead for the GH service:** host specialty, named Haemochromatosis Clinical Lead, GH MDT membership, SCI Gateway route | GPs refer to one named "D&G Haemochromatosis Service", so they don't choose between haematology and gastro. | 4 | **Primary care register and recall** for C282Y homozygotes without overload (annual ferritin + Tsat until 65) and compound heterozygotes on 12–18-month repeat | Agree a Read code and recall method; confirm it is core GMS work | Primary care / GP Sub |
| 5 | **Venesection delivery:** which phases practices do under the LES; the route for practices not in the LES; LES funding and uptake | Recommend practices do the maintenance phase (2–6 sessions/year). Sign-off needed from gastro, haematology, GP Sub committee, the Interface group,
and the LES owner (primary care contracts).

## Changes from the current local page (v1.0, reviewed 05/11/2024)

| Topic | Current local page | This draft (from the national pathway) |
|---|---|---|
| Tsat threshold for HFE testing | >50% men, >40% women | >45% for all, early-morning fasting |
| HFE testing with normal ferritin | Allowed if Tsat very high | Not recommended; needs both ferritin and Tsat raised |
| Timing of ferritin test | Not stated | Not during, or within 6 weeks of, acute illness |
| Referral destination | C282Y homozygotes to haematology; liver disease to gastro | One GH service (decision 1) |
| C282Y homozygotes without overload | Refer | Primary care: annual ferritin + Tsat until 65 |
| H63D homozygotes | Refer | Not GH; no referral |
| Compound heterozygotes | Refer | Primary care unless ferritin ≥1000 despite lifestyle change |
| Ferritin >1000 | Refer urgently to haematology | Refer if persistent and unexplained (decisions 2, 3) |
| Venesection | Practices in the LES; weekly depletion | Personalised plan, weekly or fortnightly; FBC each session; Hb <120 reduce, <110 pause |
| Maintenance target | Ferritin 50–100 | Within reference range; 50–100 only in cirrhosis |
| SNBTS donation | Not mentioned | Self-referral if never venesected; service referral in maintenance |
| Review at 65, MDT, contraindications, HCC surveillance | Not mentioned | Included |

The following are kept from the local page: the HFE request process (EDTA to
biochemistry, "Haemochromatosis genotype", relative details), and the statement
that no genetic counselling is needed.

## Sources appraised

**National Genetic Haemochromatosis pathway — CfSD Liver Specialty Delivery Group, Right Decisions (primary source)**
- Setting: NHS Scotland; integrated primary care, secondary care and SNBTS pathway.
- Thresholds, referral criteria, the primary care management of compound
  heterozygotes and non-overloaded homozygotes, venesection parameters, maintenance
  targets, SNBTS eligibility, the MDT model and the workforce model are all taken
  directly from this document.
- Notes: the fibrosis cut-offs for FibroScan, ELF and 2D-SWE are not validated in
  GH (the pathway says so itself). Maintenance targets are largely expert opinion.
  The national model delivers venesection in secondary care; GP-practice venesection
  in this draft is a local variation.

**CfSD letter to Boards, 23/09/2026**
- Asks boards to implement the national pathway or adapt existing local
  pathways. Sent for action to gastroenterology/hepatology, haematology, GPs and
  practice nurses. Does not name a lead specialty.

**Current DGRefHelp page "High ferritin / haemochromatosis" v1.0 (Heydtmann, Thomas; approved GP Sub committee and Interface group)**
- Source for the local HFE request process and the existence of a venesection LES.

**Supporting references (as cited by the national pathway)**
- BSH 2018 update (Br J Haematol 2018;181:293–303); EASL CPG 2022 (J Hepatol
  2022;77:479–502); UK Biobank HFE outcomes at 80 (BMJ Open 2024;14:e081926);
  BSG/BASL SIG venesection best practice (Frontline Gastroenterol 2025); BMJ 2023
  raised ferritin in primary care.

## Editorial uncertainties to confirm

- The baseline "common causes" bloods in Assessment (FBC, U&E, LFT, CRP, HbA1c,
  lipids) are a pragmatic list. The national pathway points to the BMJ article and
  does not give a list. Confirm with the lab and the specialists.
- Confirm that D&G biochemistry reports Tsat, and whether it can add an interpretive
  comment to HFE results that points to this pathway.
- Confirm that the venesection LES is still active, and its current specification,
  before the LES wording is finalised.
- Units: the national pathway uses ng/mL; the old local page used µg/L. They are
  numerically equivalent. Match whichever unit the D&G lab reports.
