Background

Most raised ferritin is not iron overload. Only around 10% of patients with a raised serum ferritin have iron overload; the usual causes are alcohol, metabolic syndrome / MASLD, inflammation, infection and malignancy. Ferritin is only acted on as possible iron overload when the transferrin saturation (Tsat) is also raised (>45%).

Genetic haemochromatosis (GH) in this pathway means iron overload in a patient who is homozygous for p.C282Y in the HFE gene. Diagnosis requires both the genotype and biochemical iron overload — penetrance is variable (serious iron overload in around 30–35% of male and 5–10% of female C282Y homozygotes). H63D homozygotes and carriers of a single mutation are not at risk of clinically significant iron overload. C282Y/H63D compound heterozygosity is not disease-causing on its own.

Most patients are managed in primary care. Refer only C282Y homozygotes with biochemical iron overload, and patients with unexplained ferritin persistently ≥1000 ng/mL despite addressing lifestyle factors. Early treatment of GH prevents cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma and allows normal life expectancy.
