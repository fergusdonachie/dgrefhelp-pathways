1. Raised ferritin — not GH (Tsat ≤45%, or HFE genotype other than C282Y/C282Y)

  • Take a detailed alcohol history and assess for metabolic syndrome.
  • Investigate abnormal LFTs with a full non-invasive liver screen, and offer BBV testing where there are risk factors.
  • Calculate FIB-4 where there is MASLD or alcohol risk. FIB-4 >1.3 (>2.0 if aged over 65): follow the D&G liver fibrosis pathway. [TO AGREE 8: link to the local liver fibrosis / FibroScan pathway]
  • Give lifestyle advice: alcohol reduction, weight management, smoking cessation.
  • Repeat iron studies after 12–18 months. If ferritin is ≤500 ng/mL and there is no advanced fibrosis, no further follow-up is needed.
  • Ferritin persistently ≥1000 ng/mL despite effective lifestyle change and with no clear explanation: refer (see Who to refer).

Patients with a clear alternative explanation (for example alcohol excess or metabolic syndrome) do not need referral.

2. C282Y/H63D compound heterozygote

  • No biochemical iron overload: reassure and discharge. Give liver health advice. The patient may self-refer to SNBTS to donate blood.
  • Ferritin above URR but <1000 ng/mL: manage as for raised ferritin above (alcohol, metabolic syndrome, FIB-4, lifestyle, repeat at 12–18 months). No referral.
  • Ferritin ≥1000 ng/mL despite effective lifestyle change: refer.

3. C282Y homozygote WITHOUT biochemical iron overload — manage in primary care; do not refer.

  • Offer family screening (below).
  • Advise avoiding iron and vitamin C supplements.
  • Tell them about blood donation: patients who have never been venesected can self-refer to SNBTS (Scotblood).
  • Annual ferritin and Tsat until age 65. Refer if iron overload develops. [TO AGREE 4: practice recall method / Read code for this register]
  • At 65, review whether monitoring is still worthwhile, based on frailty, co-morbidity and likely benefit.

Family screening

  • Offer ferritin, Tsat and HFE genotype to adult (≥18) siblings and children of a C282Y homozygote with GH.
  • Test parents only after an individual decision based on age, sex and ferritin.
  • Relatives are usually asymptomatic. Their GP requests the tests. [TO AGREE 9: local patient leaflet to give to relatives]
