After referral, every new patient is seen in a nurse-led GH clinic (fibrosis assessment, baseline bloods, contraindication check) and discussed at the GH MDT. The MDT decides whether venesection is likely to help. Frail, elderly or highly co-morbid patients may not benefit. Who delivers venesection

The national pathway uses a secondary care, nurse-led service. In D&G, venesection may be done in GP practices under the venesection LES, working to a written specialist plan. This is a local variation from the national pathway, because of the region’s rural geography. Specialist plan — issued by the GH service before practice venesection starts

  • Volume (usually 450–500 mL) and frequency (weekly or fortnightly)
  • Target ferritin and date of the next ferritin check
  • Any B12/folate replacement
  • Named contact for results and advice [TO AGREE 6: escalation route — GH nurse phone line / shared inbox; who reviews ferritin results and updates the plan]

At each venesection session

  • Check for temporary contraindications: systolic BP <100 mmHg, intercurrent illness, pregnancy, stroke in the last 6 months, iron-deficiency anaemia, poor venous access. If present, defer and inform the GH service.
  • FBC at every session.
  • Hb <120 g/L — reduce frequency. Hb <110 g/L — pause and inform the GH service.

Permanent contraindications (e.g. active cancer, terminal illness, severe or uncontrolled cardiac disease) are reviewed by the GH MDT.

Depletion phase

  • 450–500 mL weekly or fortnightly, following a personalised plan.
  • Ferritin after every 4–6 sessions until it falls to 200 ng/mL, then every 1–2 sessions.
  • Target ferritin 50–100 ng/mL. A ferritin within the reference range may be accepted by agreement between the patient and the team.
  • Patients in the depletion phase should avoid raw or undercooked shellfish (Vibrio vulnificus risk).

Maintenance phase

  • Usually 2–6 venesections a year; FBC at each session, ferritin every 6 months.
  • Target ferritin within the reference range (<300 ng/mL men and post-menopausal women; <200 ng/mL pre-menopausal women).
  • Cirrhosis: stays in secondary care for venesection (target 50–100 ng/mL) and 6-monthly ultrasound and AFP.
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia or an unexplained fall in venesection need: consider blood loss and investigate as usual.
  • Annual review in the nurse-led GH clinic (face to face, virtual, telephone or patient-initiated). Agree the plan and target ferritin for the year ahead. [TO AGREE 7: service capacity to provide annual review for all patients, including those venesected in practice or donating]
  • At 65, the MDT reviews whether to continue. Patients with no end-organ damage and a life expectancy under 10 years may stop. Anyone continuing is reviewed every 1–2 years.

Blood donation (SNBTS)

  • Stable maintenance-phase patients can be referred by the GH service to SNBTS, using the SNBTS GH referral form. To be eligible they need no end-organ damage, good venous access, no adverse reactions, and no need for IV fluids.
  • Donation is at most every 12 weeks, with Hb 125–165 g/L (women) or 135–180 g/L (men). The usual upper age limit of 65 can be waived at SNBTS discretion.
  • SNBTS does not notify the GH team. Patients must report their donations at annual review.
  • Donation is always optional. Patients who cannot or do not want to donate continue with service or practice venesection.
