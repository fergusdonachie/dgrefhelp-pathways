When to check ferritin and iron studies

  • Chronic unexplained fatigue or weakness
  • Early-onset arthralgia or atypical arthropathy (typically 2nd/3rd MCP joints and ankles)
  • Abnormal LFTs or liver disease of unknown cause — ferritin and Tsat are part of the non-invasive liver screen and iLFT protocol
  • Less common: early-onset erectile dysfunction, early menopause or loss of libido; early-onset arrhythmia or cardiomyopathy; unexplained increasing skin pigmentation
  • Family screening: adult (≥18 years) siblings and children of a C282Y homozygote with GH (see Primary care management)

Step 1 — serum ferritin, when the patient is well. Do not test during acute illness or for 6 weeks afterwards. Use the local laboratory reference range.

No further iron tests needed if ferritin is:

  • <200 ng/mL in pre-menopausal women
  • <300 ng/mL in men and post-menopausal women
  • or below the upper reference range (URR) of the local laboratory

Step 2 — early-morning fasting transferrin saturation (Tsat) if ferritin is above these levels. Ferritin and Tsat can be requested together or in sequence.

At the same time, look for the common causes of a raised ferritin: FBC, U&E, LFTs, CRP, HbA1c, lipids, alcohol history (units/week), BMI.

Step 3 — HFE genotype only when there is biochemical iron overload:

Request HFE genotype if Tsat >45% AND

  • ferritin >200 ng/mL in pre-menopausal women, or
  • ferritin >300 ng/mL in men and post-menopausal women, or
  • ferritin above the local laboratory URR

Do not request HFE testing when Tsat is ≤45%, or when ferritin is normal.

How to request: EDTA sample to biochemistry marked “Haemochromatosis genotype”. For family screening, give the affected relative’s name, DOB, health board and the family mutation if known. No genetic counselling or referral to Clinical Genetics is needed.

Interpreting the genotype

  • C282Y/C282Y (homozygote) — GH if biochemical iron overload is present. See Who to refer / Primary care management.
  • C282Y/H63D (compound heterozygote) — not disease-causing on its own; mild iron overload usually reflects alcohol or metabolic syndrome. Manage in primary care.
  • H63D/H63D, single C282Y or H63D (carrier), or no mutation — not at risk of clinically significant iron overload from HFE. Manage the raised ferritin as for any other cause.
