Refer to the D&G Haemochromatosis Service [TO AGREE 1: host specialty (gastroenterology/hepatology or haematology), named clinical lead, and SCI Gateway referral route]

  • C282Y homozygote with biochemical iron overload (TIBC saturation >45% and ferritin above the thresholds in Assessment).
  • C282Y/H63D compound heterozygote with ferritin ≥1000 ng/mL despite effective lifestyle change.

Ferritin ≥1000 ng/mL in a C282Y homozygote indicates a higher risk of liver fibrosis. Include this in the referral; the specialist service will assess fibrosis risk.

Referral priority

  • Routine for all referrals, including ferritin ≥1000 ng/mL.
  • Urgent only for severe end-organ damage, e.g. heart failure or cardiomyopathy, or arrhythmia that is difficult to control.
  • Ferritin level alone does not determine urgency.

Unexplained ferritin persistently ≥1000 ng/mL without a GH genotype, despite effective lifestyle change and with no clear alternative cause

Refer for specialist evaluation (possible MRI liver iron quantification, testing for rare HFE or non-HFE iron overload). [TO AGREE 2: receiving specialty — haematology, or the Haemochromatosis Service]

Refer via the existing liver pathway (not this pathway)

  • Any patient with FIB-4 >1.3 (>2.0 if over 65), cirrhosis, or signs of chronic liver disease — follow the D&G liver pathway, whatever the genotype.

Include in the referral: HFE genotype, ferritin and TIBC saturation (with dates, including previous results), FBC, LFTs, HbA1c, FIB-4, ASSIGN 2.0 score, alcohol intake, BMI, and relevant co-morbidity.

While waiting, patients can be signposted to the British Liver Trust — haemochromatosis.
