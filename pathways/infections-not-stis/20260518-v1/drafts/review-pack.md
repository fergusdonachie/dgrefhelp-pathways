## Key clinical decisions captured

### STI risk stratification
- Binary (high/low risk) matching QURIS flowchart criteria
- High-risk criteria trigger examination and NAAT regardless of symptom pattern
- Syndromic treatment is appropriate for lower-risk women with characteristic symptoms

### BV management
- First-line: metronidazole 400mg BD for 5–7 days (BASHH preferred; better completion than 2g stat)
- 2g stat retained as alternative (convenience, short course)
- Topical: metronidazole gel 0.75% daily for 5 days — useful in pregnancy (avoid systemic 2g stat)
- Clindamycin vaginal cream 2% for 7 days — alternative if metronidazole not tolerated
- Tinidazole 2g stat — effective alternative to metronidazole
- Pregnancy: avoid oral metronidazole 2g stat; use 400mg BD for 5–7 days or topical metronidazole gel; avoid clindamycin cream in first trimester (latex damage)
- Recurrent BV (≥3 episodes/year): suppressive metronidazole gel 0.75% twice weekly for 16 weeks (off-licence); consider lactic acid gel (Relactagel) for pH maintenance

### Candidiasis management
- First-line: fluconazole 150mg oral stat OR clotrimazole 500mg pessary stat (equal efficacy)
- Pregnancy: avoid oral fluconazole (teratogenicity risk — avoid in first trimester, caution throughout); use clotrimazole topically
- Recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis (RVVC): defined as ≥4 symptomatic episodes in 12 months; induction with fluconazole 150mg every 72 hours x3 doses, then maintenance fluconazole 150mg weekly for 6 months
- Non-albicans Candida (e.g. C. glabrata): may not respond to fluconazole — consider HVS for speciation, refer if refractory

### Pregnancy notes
- Avoid oral fluconazole throughout pregnancy (risk of cardiac defects — FDA/MHRA signal; use only if benefit outweighs risk)
- Use clotrimazole 500mg pessary or cream topically
- BV treatment indicated in symptomatic pregnant women (associated with preterm birth); metronidazole 400mg BD x5–7 days is preferred systemic option
- HVS recommended to confirm diagnosis in pregnancy

### Who to refer
- PID — urgent (same-day or 999 if systemically unwell)
- Post-menopausal new discharge — red flag for malignancy; 2-week wait referral
- Failed treatment / diagnostic uncertainty — sexual health clinic
- Suspected foreign body — gynaecology or examination under anaesthetic if not retrievable
- Recurrent RVVC not responding to maintenance therapy — gynaecology / GU medicine

## Editorial uncertainties

1. **Pregnancy and fluconazole:** Current MHRA/NICE position is to avoid oral fluconazole in pregnancy. Some sources say first trimester only; NICE CKS (pre-403 knowledge) advises avoid throughout. Draft takes conservative position — avoid throughout, use topical clotrimazole. **Review against current SmPC and MHRA guidance.**

2. **Recurrent BV suppressive therapy:** Metronidazole gel 0.75% twice weekly for 16 weeks is off-licence but included in patient.info and referenced in BASHH context. Astodrimer gel (Betafem) is licensed for recurrent BV prevention but may not be on D&G formulary. **Check D&G/GGC formulary before publishing.**

3. **Trichomonas vaginalis:** Included in differential table (frothy yellow-green discharge) but management deferred to sexual health clinic as it is technically an STI. **Confirm this is appropriate scope for this pathway.**

4. **Clindamycin vaginal cream in pregnancy:** Avoid in first trimester (damages latex condoms/diaphragms; theoretical systemic absorption concerns). **Check current BASHH pregnancy guidance.**

5. **NAAT self-sampling:** Draft states self-taken swabs available for chlamydia/gonorrhoea in community (D&G context). **Confirm current D&G/DGNH sexual health service provision.**

6. **Lactic acid gel (Relactagel):** Not clearly listed in BNF as a named drug — included without BNF link. **Check current BNF/MIMS status before publishing.**
