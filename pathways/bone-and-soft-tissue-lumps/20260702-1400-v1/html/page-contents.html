Bone and soft tissue lumps: assessment and suspected sarcoma referral

Sarcomas are rare but the cost of a missed one is high. The rule of thumb: any soft tissue lump that is >5 cm, deep to fascia, growing, painful or recurrent needs urgent ultrasound; unexplained non-mechanical bone pain needs an X-ray.

  • Suspicious soft tissue lump → urgent ultrasound; suspicious/uncertain scan → urgent suspicion-of-cancer referral to the sarcoma service.
  • X-ray suggesting bone tumour → urgent discussion/referral — do not biopsy outside the specialist centre.
  • Small, superficial, soft, stable lumps (typical lipomata) and classic wrist ganglia can be managed in primary care.
