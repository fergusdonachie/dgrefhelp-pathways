Investigation of male hypogonadism (testosterone deficiency)

Confirm the diagnosis before referring: hypogonadism requires both symptoms and a genuinely low testosterone. Avoid investigating men with vague symptoms during acute reversible illness.

  • First test — total testosterone, 0800–0900h, fasted, brown tube (reference range 10–30 nmol/L). Symptoms that prompt testing: low libido, erectile dysfunction, and loss of early morning erections.
  • Always confirm a low or equivocal result. Repeat at 4 weeks (fasted, 0800–0900h) with bioavailable testosterone, LH/FSH, prolactin, TSH and ferritin.
  • >11 nmol/L → androgen deficiency unlikely, referral not indicated. <7 nmol/L confirmed → refer endocrinology once illness and drug effects are excluded. 7–11 nmol/L confirmed → refer only if clearly symptomatic and not improving; otherwise repeat in 6 months.
  • Review drugs and lifestyle first. Opiates, glucocorticoids, anabolic steroids, antipsychotics and alcohol excess all lower testosterone; 5–10% weight loss can improve levels in milder deficiency.
