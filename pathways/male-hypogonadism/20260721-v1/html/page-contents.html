Investigation of male hypogonadism (testosterone deficiency)

A primary care approach to confirming testosterone deficiency and deciding who needs endocrinology — most low results do not require referral.

  • First test — total testosterone (0800–0900h, brown tube; reference 10–30 nmol/L) in men with reduced libido, erectile dysfunction or loss of early-morning erections.
  • Confirm every low or equivocal result — repeat at 4 weeks (fasted, 0800–0900h) with bioavailable testosterone, LH/FSH, prolactin, TSH and ferritin.
  • >11 nmol/L → referral not indicated. <7 confirmed → refer endocrinology once illness and drugs are excluded. 7–11 confirmed → refer only if clearly symptomatic and not improving; otherwise repeat in 6 months.
  • Review drugs and lifestyle first — opiates, glucocorticoids, anabolic steroids, antipsychotics and alcohol lower testosterone; a 5–10% weight loss may improve levels.
