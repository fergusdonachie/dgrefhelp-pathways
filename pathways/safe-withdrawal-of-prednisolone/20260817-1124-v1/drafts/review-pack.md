# Safe withdrawal of long-term prednisolone in adults - review pack

**Run:** 20260817-1124-v1

**Audience:** NHS Dumfries & Galloway GPs, ANPs, pharmacists and outpatient clinicians

**Status:** Draft for local Endocrinology, Biochemistry, pharmacy and editorial review

**Date:** 17 August 2026

## Purpose

This draft converts the supplied two-page guidance into a practical DGRefHelp prescribing and safety page for withdrawal of long-term prednisolone prescribed for non-endocrine disease. It is intentionally structured as a clinical guide rather than a referral pathway.

## Page structure

1. Scope and exclusions
2. Checks before starting the taper
3. Dose-by-dose tapering schedule
4. Clinical taper or adrenal testing at 5 mg daily
5. Distinguishing withdrawal, adrenal insufficiency and disease flare
6. Sick-day rules and emergency action
7. Limited indications for specialist advice

## Material drafting decisions

- The supplied taper table is retained and presented as an individualised starting framework, not a fixed prescription.
- Withdrawal starts only when the underlying condition is controlled and prednisolone is no longer required; disease-specific or specialist plans take precedence.
- Routine adrenal testing is deferred until the dose is physiological (approximately 4-6 mg prednisolone daily), in line with the 2024 joint endocrine guideline.
- The page offers either a slow clinical taper or morning cortisol testing at 5 mg daily. Short Synacthen testing is not presented as routine.
- The supplied morning cortisol and post-Synacthen thresholds are retained in a clearly labelled proposed-local table because they match the NHS GGC 2024 protocol but differ from the broader 2024 joint guideline at the lower morning-cortisol boundary.
- Routine conversion from prednisolone to hydrocortisone is not recommended. It remains an option when small prednisolone reductions are impracticable or after specialist advice.
- Steroid Emergency Card, sick-day advice and emergency recognition are given their own safety section.
- Routine Endocrinology referral is avoided; specific advice thresholds are provided instead.

## Points requiring local confirmation before publication

1. NHS D&G Endocrinology and Biochemistry approval of the morning cortisol bands: below 100, 100-300 and above 300 nmol/L.
2. Approval of the post-Synacthen bands: below 250, 250-430 and above 430 nmol/L, including sampling protocol and assay.
3. Required interval between the last prednisolone dose and morning cortisol sampling. The supplied draft says to omit the morning dose; the 2024 joint guideline advises holding glucocorticoid for at least 24 hours.
4. Which clinicians can request or arrange a short Synacthen test and the exact local route for advice.
5. The approved D&G sick-day doses, peri-operative cover, Steroid Emergency Card supply route and duration of precautions after stopping.
6. Whether 1 mg prednisolone tablets are the preferred method for the final taper and whether hydrocortisone conversion should remain on the page.
7. Author, reviewer, version, governance approval and review date.
