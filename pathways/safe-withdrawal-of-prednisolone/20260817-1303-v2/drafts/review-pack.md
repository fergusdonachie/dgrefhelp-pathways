# Safe withdrawal of prednisolone therapy in adults with non-endocrine disease - V2 review pack

**Run:** 20260817-1303-v2

**Status:** Draft for local Endocrinology, Biochemistry, pharmacy and editorial review

**Date:** 17 August 2026

## V2 approach

V2 deliberately follows the supplied two-page Word document much more closely than V1. It retains the document's sequence and clinical content, with only minor spelling and grammar corrections and web formatting.

The five page sections are:

1. Background
2. Suggested prednisolone taper
3. Key messages
4. Who should have adrenal function assessed?
5. Adrenal function testing and action

The source document's scope statement is placed above the accordions because it determines whether the guidance applies. The list of patients requiring assessment is given its own accordion to make it easier to find; its position in the sequence is unchanged.

## Changes from the Word document

- Corrected typographical and grammatical errors without changing the intended meaning.
- Presented the taper and test interpretation as tables.
- Clarified that the guideline table is expressed as a prednisone/prednisolone-equivalent daily dose. The source Word document describes prednisolone throughout but its table heading uses prednisone equivalent.
- Added a visible editorial warning that the supplied cortisol and short Synacthen test thresholds need confirmation against the D&G assay and local testing protocol before publication.
- Linked the 2024 joint European Society of Endocrinology and Endocrine Society guideline named in the source.

## Material from V1 not carried into V2

To keep V2 close to the Word document, it does not retain the additional V1 sections on pre-taper checks, differential diagnosis of symptoms, detailed adrenal-crisis management, specialist referral criteria, or the expanded resource list. These can be reintroduced selectively if local reviewers want them.

## Points requiring local confirmation before publication

1. Confirm the proposed early-morning cortisol bands of below 100, 100-300 and above 300 nmol/L with D&G Endocrinology and Biochemistry. The 2024 joint guideline gives generic guide bands of below 150, 150-300 and above 300 nmol/L.
2. Confirm the proposed post-Synacthen cortisol bands of below 250, 250-430 and above 430 nmol/L, including the local assay and sampling protocol. The joint guideline recommends morning cortisol as the first test and advises against routine dynamic testing during withdrawal.
3. Confirm the instruction to omit only the morning prednisolone dose before testing. The joint guideline describes morning cortisol testing after glucocorticoid has been held for at least 24 hours, excluding dexamethasone.
4. Confirm the local sick-day-rule wording, Steroid Emergency Card arrangements and any patient-information link to include.
5. Add author, approver, version and review date after governance approval.
