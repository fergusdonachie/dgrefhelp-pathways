## Editorial Uncertainties / Queries

### 1. Endometrial thickness threshold — MOST IMPORTANT
- NICE CKS (could not retrieve) typically uses **≥4mm** as the threshold for referral in women NOT on HRT
- RCOG uses ≤4mm as normal for postmenopausal women not on HRT (i.e. >4mm = abnormal)
- The existing QURIS pathway cites **>7mm** for asymptomatic findings — this appears to apply to incidental findings in women NOT presenting with PMB
- This pathway has been written using the **>4mm threshold for symptomatic PMB** and **>7mm for asymptomatic incidental finding** — please verify with Heather Currie / Gynaecology
- Women on HRT: a higher threshold may apply (some guidelines use >8mm on sequential HRT) — flagged but not specified in body text

### 2. NICE NG12 age threshold
- NG12 1.5.12 specifies 2WW for women **aged 55 and over**; 1.5.14 adds women **under 55** with unexplained PMB
- In practice D&G refers ALL PMB on 2WW regardless of age — this is stated clearly in the pathway and is consistent with NG12 read as a whole

### 3. HRT unscheduled bleeding
- NICE NG12 1.5.15 specifically notes limited evidence for unscheduled bleeding on HRT and directs to British Menopause Society (BMS) guidance
- The "Who not to refer" section includes a tightly-written exception for well-documented withdrawal bleeds on sequential HRT — please confirm scope of this exception with gynaecology before publishing

### 4. HNPCC/Lynch syndrome
- Included as a specific referral trigger (same 2WW pathway) per QURIS source
- Lynch syndrome increases endometrial cancer risk ~40–60% lifetime — standard of care is to refer at first presentation of PMB

### 5. West vs East pathway names
- WEST: One-stop PMB clinic — DGRI/Mountainhall — scan + gynaecology review in same visit
- EAST: Refer to GCH Gynaecology; scan arranged separately
- Please confirm current clinic names and SCI referral destination codes before publishing

## Key Clinical Content Summary

### Definition
Any vaginal bleeding occurring ≥12 months after the last menstrual period.

### Cancer risk
~10% of women presenting with PMB have endometrial cancer. All PMB must be investigated to exclude malignancy.

### Causes (approximate frequency)
- Atrophic vaginitis / urogenital atrophy — most common (~60%)
- Endometrial atrophy
- Endometrial polyps
- Endometrial hyperplasia (with or without atypia)
- Endometrial carcinoma (~10%)
- Cervical pathology (polyps, carcinoma)
- HRT-related (unscheduled or breakthrough bleeding)
- Anticoagulant medication
- Submucous fibroid
- Vaginal pathology

### Investigation pathway
1. TV-USS — first-line; endometrial thickness measurement
2. Endometrial biopsy (Pipelle) — if endometrium ≥4mm or technically possible
3. Hysteroscopy ± biopsy — if TV-USS inadequate or biopsy inconclusive

### Referral thresholds (NICE NG12)
- ALL unexplained PMB → 2WW suspected cancer referral
- Asymptomatic ET >4mm (not on HRT): refer to gynaecology (routine or urgent — see editorial note)
- Asymptomatic ET >7mm (used in existing QURIS pathway): routine gynaecology
- HNPCC/Lynch syndrome + any PMB: 2WW referral

## Section Content (pre-HTML prose)

### Background
PMB is defined as any vaginal bleeding occurring ≥12 months after the last menstrual period. It affects up to 10% of postmenopausal women and requires investigation in all cases to exclude endometrial cancer, which is found in approximately 10% of women presenting with PMB. The most common cause is atrophic vaginitis or urogenital atrophy (~60%), but malignancy cannot be assumed to be absent without investigation.

### Primary care assessment
Full history including: onset, frequency, quantity; HRT use (type, duration, last dose change — distinguish scheduled withdrawal bleed from unscheduled bleeding); anticoagulant medications (warfarin, DOACs); cervical screening status; personal or family history of HNPCC/Lynch syndrome. Examination: abdominal palpation; speculum examination to inspect cervix and vaginal walls (note signs of atrophy, polyps, contact bleeding, visible lesion); bimanual palpation if appropriate. Smear only if due — do NOT delay referral to perform smear.

### Primary care management
Very limited role for primary care management before investigation is complete. Can offer topical oestrogen for confirmed symptomatic urogenital atrophy — but do NOT delay referral to trial treatment first. If patient is on sequential HRT with unscheduled bleeding, review HRT dose/type but still refer for investigation.

### Who to refer
ALL PMB warrants 2WW referral (NICE NG12). D&G pathway: West → one-stop PMB clinic DGRI/Mountainhall; East → GCH Gynaecology. HNPCC/Lynch syndrome → 2WW. Asymptomatic endometrial thickness >7mm on incidental scan → routine gynaecology referral.

### Who not to refer
Very limited exceptions. Possible exception: well-documented isolated spotting in expected withdrawal window on correctly-taken sequential HRT, with normal recent endometrial scan. Even in this scenario, low threshold to refer if uncertainty exists or bleeding pattern changes.
