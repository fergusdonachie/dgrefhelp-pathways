Energy Limiting Conditions (ME/CFS, Long COVID, Fibromyalgia)

Energy limiting conditions (ELCs) are long-term conditions where fatigue and energy impairment are the defining features, limiting the overall amount and pace of activity a person can do, their daily function and quality of life.

Conditions covered by this pathway:

  • Myalgic Encephalomyelitis / Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (ME/CFS) — profound fatigue, sleep disturbance and post-exertional malaise
  • Long COVID — persistent, debilitating symptoms following SARS-CoV-2 infection
  • Fibromyalgia — widespread musculoskeletal pain with significant fatigue and fluctuating symptoms

Key characteristics:

  • Energy impairment — more than tiredness; a severe lack of energy that limits basic tasks
  • Fluctuating symptoms — severity may vary hourly, daily or weekly
  • Post-exertional malaise (PEM) — marked worsening after minimal physical, cognitive or emotional exertion
  • Cognitive dysfunction ("brain fog") — problems with memory, concentration and processing

PEM changes management. Symptom worsening after exertion is often delayed by 24–48 hours and recovery is prolonged. If PEM is present, do not advise patients to "push through" or exercise their way out of symptoms.

Making the diagnosis in primary care

These are clinical diagnoses — GPs can and should make them. There is no diagnostic test and no requirement for specialist confirmation. A clear, positively framed diagnosis is itself therapeutic, prevents repeated investigation, and is the entry requirement for the ELC pathway.

Step 1 — History and examination

  • Fatigue pattern: relationship to activity, effect of rest, delayed worsening after exertion (PEM)
  • Sleep quality (unrefreshing sleep), cognitive symptoms, pain distribution
  • For long COVID: timeline from acute infection; symptom list is broad (breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness, chest tightness, brain fog)
  • Impact on function: work, self-care, mobility
  • Screen for mimics and contributors: depression/anxiety, sleep apnoea (snoring, BMI, Epworth), menopause, alcohol, medication causes (beta-blockers, statins, sedating drugs)

Step 2 — One blood panel excludes the common alternatives (all three conditions)

  • FBC, U&E, LFT, TFT, CRP (and/or plasma viscosity), calcium & phosphate, HbA1c, ferritin, coeliac screen, urinalysis
  • Add CK if widespread pain or muscle weakness
  • Consider if indicated: vitamin D, B12/folate, BNP (breathless post-COVID), HIV/hepatitis serology

Normal results plus a typical clinical picture = make the diagnosis. Do not serially re-investigate stable, typical symptoms.

Step 3 — Apply the condition-specific criteria

Simplified diagnostic criteria
ME/CFS (NICE NG206)
  • All 4 must be present:
    • Debilitating fatigue, worsened by activity, not relieved by rest
    • Post-exertional malaise (often delayed 24–48h)
    • Unrefreshing sleep
    • Cognitive difficulties
  • Suspect at 6 weeks of symptoms
  • Diagnose at 3 months if not explained by another condition
Long COVID (NICE NG188)
  • Signs/symptoms continuing 4–12 weeks after acute COVID-19 = ongoing symptomatic COVID-19
  • Beyond 12 weeks, not explained by an alternative diagnosis = post-COVID-19 syndrome
  • Targeted checks:
    • 1-minute sit-to-stand test with oxygen saturations (desaturation ≥3% is abnormal)
    • Lying & standing BP/HR if dizziness or palpitations on standing
    • CXR by 12 weeks if ongoing respiratory symptoms
Fibromyalgia (RCP 2022)
  • Pain in ≥4 of 5 body regions (4 quadrants + axial)
  • Present ≥3 months at a similar level
  • With fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and cognitive symptoms
  • Score it with the RCP diagnostic worksheet: WPI ≥7 + SSS ≥5, or WPI 4–6 + SSS ≥9
  • Diagnosis is valid alongside other conditions (e.g. inflammatory arthritis)

Stop — features suggesting another diagnosis (investigate/refer instead):

  • Weight loss, fever, night sweats, lymphadenopathy
  • Focal neurological signs
  • Inflammatory features: joint swelling, early morning stiffness >1 hour, persistently raised inflammatory markers
  • Cardiac-sounding chest pain
  • Hypoxaemia or oxygen desaturation on exertion — urgent referral (NG188)
  • Significant suicidality or severe self-neglect

Communicating the diagnosis

  • Name the condition and code it — avoid "we can't find anything wrong"
  • Explain fluctuation and PEM so the patient can interpret their own symptoms
  • Give a patient resource at the point of diagnosis (see Resources)

Primary care management

Energy management (all ELCs)

  • Pacing: plan activity within the current energy envelope, with rest built in — the aim is stability, not pushing limits
  • Do not offer graded exercise therapy for ME/CFS (explicitly removed in NICE NG206)
  • For long COVID, increase activity only as tolerated; if PEM is present, manage as for ME/CFS

Symptom management

  • Sleep: routine sleep advice, but set expectations — sleep is often unrefreshing despite good habits
  • Fibromyalgia pain: non-drug approaches first (education, activity within limits, psychology-informed approaches). If a drug trial is appropriate, consider low-dose Amitriptyline at night (off-label). Avoid opioids; do not start gabapentinoids routinely
  • Orthostatic symptoms: fluid and salt advice, slow postural change; check lying/standing BP and HR
  • Treat coexisting depression/anxiety on their own merits — they are common consequences, not the cause

Practical support

  • Review and rationalise medications contributing to fatigue
  • Work/education: fit notes supporting phased return, amended duties, or time off during relapse; signpost occupational health
  • Regular planned review — particularly for severe ME/CFS (housebound/bedbound patients may need home visits)

Who to refer

Refer to the NHS D&G ELC Pathway when ALL of the following apply:

  • Adult over 16
  • Symptoms persisting >12 weeks
  • ELC diagnosis made after appropriate history and investigations (see Making the diagnosis)
  • Ongoing functional limitation requiring condition-specific assessment and intervention to improve or maintain function and quality of life
  • Medically stable
  • Able to give informed consent and engage with assessment and intervention (with appropriate support) at face-to-face, telephone or home visit appointments

Referral route: SCI Gateway → Mountainhall → Regional Rehab Service → Physio Comm Rehab (AHP Regional Rehab Team)

What the service offers: a multidisciplinary, person-centred team (Occupational Therapy, Physiotherapy, Psychology, with links to Rehabilitation Medicine) providing specialist rehabilitation approaches and supported self-management, including signposting to public health, third sector and community agencies.

Who not to refer

  • Under 16 — contact child health services instead
  • Symptoms explained by another diagnosis on history or investigation — manage or refer for that condition
  • No diagnosis yet made — complete the primary care diagnostic steps first; the pathway does not provide a diagnostic service
  • Self-managing well with support from Public Health Improvement / Community Link Workers, third sector or leisure services — signpost rather than refer
  • Medically unstable, or unable to engage with assessment and intervention even with support — address the barrier first
